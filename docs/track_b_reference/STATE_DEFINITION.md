# Exact subtree-interface automaton

Status: universal finite-state closure for proper strong 2-separation in K2.

## 1. Compact helix grammar is complete

Contract every maximal helix to one ordered tree node labelled by its length
class and by the endpoint type of its terminal pair.  The immutable interval
tree definitions and motif bounds imply exactly this grammar.

```text
Helix length: 2, 3, 4, or >=5.
Endpoint E : no unpaired child, 0 helix children.
Endpoint L0: an unpaired child, 0 helix children.
Endpoint L1: an unpaired child, 1 helix child.
Endpoint M2: no unpaired child, 2 helix children.
Endpoint M3: no unpaired child, 3 helix children.

Root with an unpaired child   : 1 or 2 helix children.
Root without an unpaired child: 1, 2, 3, or 4 helix children.
```

Why nothing is missing:

1. Along a maximal helix, each nonterminal pair has exactly its next stacked
   pair as its only paired child and has no unpaired child.
2. A terminal pair with an unpaired child has at most one paired child by
   absence of `m3dot`, giving `L0/L1`.
3. A terminal pair without an unpaired child cannot have exactly one paired
   child: the exact interval-tree characterization would make that child the
   next stacked pair, contradicting maximality.  Counts 0,2,3 give `E/M2/M3`;
   count above 3 violates `m5` after including the paired-parent incidence.
4. At the root, `m5` gives at most four paired children, and `m3dot` gives at
   most two when an unpaired child is present.  K2 is nonempty, so the count is
   at least one.

Conversely, every finite ordered tree generated by this grammar has a concrete
noncrossing realization in the locked model.  Realize each helix as nested
adjacent pairs, concatenate sibling intervals in backbone order, and insert one
singleton in every `L` loop (and at the root when that flag is present).  `E`
may use an adjacent terminal pair.  The stated degree bounds guarantee motif
avoidance, and the inserted singleton prevents an `L1` edge from merging into
the incoming maximal helix.  Therefore the grammar is neither an
over-approximation nor an under-approximation.

Track a counter `k` equal to the number of length-2 labels in a subtree.  K2
means total root counter exactly two; length 1 is absent and every other label
is at least 3.

## 2. Boundary state

Fix global residues `xi` and `eta=xi+1`.  For a helix-subtree `H`, define

```text
Accept(H) subseteq {xi,eta} x {B,W,G}.
```

`(r,c)` belongs to `Accept(H)` exactly when the complete concrete subtree can
be coloured so that:

- the outer helix enters at residue `r` and its first pair has colour `c`;
- every exposure inside the subtree is proper;
- every grey pair is at residue `eta`;
- every unpaired node in the subtree is at residue `xi`.

The bit order in the JSON certificate is

```text
xi:B, xi:W, xi:G, eta:B, eta:W, eta:G.
```

## 3. Exact recurrence

For each boundary `(r,c)`, range over every exact helix transfer

```text
(r,c) -> (t,a),
```

where `t` is the terminal residue and `a` the closing colour.  Retain the
boundary exactly in the following cases.

- `E`: always; `{inv(a)}` is proper and there is no child.
- `L0`: exactly when `t=xi`.
- `L1`: exactly when `t=xi` and some port `p` satisfies
  `(xi,p) in Accept(child)` and `{inv(a),p}` is proper.
- `Md`, `d=2,3`: exactly when `t=eta` and there are ports `p1,...,pd` with
  `(eta,pi) in Accept(child_i)` and
  `{inv(a),p1,...,pd}` proper.

This recurrence is exact in both directions.

**Soundness.**  A retained helix word supplies every internal exposure and the
correct terminal residue.  A retained port tuple supplies the terminal
exposure.  The child certificates supply disjoint smaller subtrees and preserve
their assigned first colours.  Gluing can introduce no other exposure or level
constraint.

**Completeness.**  Restrict any real colouring to the incoming helix.  Its word
occurs in the exact helix relation.  The endpoint taxonomy fixes one of the
five cases above.  The real first colours of the actual children form one of
the enumerated proper port tuples, and restriction to each child proves the
corresponding membership in its exact `Accept` state.  Hence the recurrence
retains the original boundary.

No constraint crosses between sibling subtrees except their common parent
exposure, which the port tuple records completely.  This proves that the
six-bit state loses no information relevant to strong 2-separation.

## 4. Reachable closure for k <= 2

The least fixed point contains only

```text
F = {xi:B, xi:W, eta:B, eta:W, eta:G}  (bitset 59),
Q = {xi:B, xi:W, eta:G}                 (bitset 35).
```

Its exact stratification is

| short count `k` | reachable states |
|---:|---|
| 0 | `F` |
| 1 | `F`, `Q` |
| 2 | `F`, `Q` |

Both states in the latter rows are genuinely reachable; they are not merely
abstract supersets.

### Human closure proof

Use the following explicit induction invariant:

```text
I(H): if k(H)=0 then Accept(H)=F;
      if k(H) is 1 or 2 then Accept(H) is F or Q;
      and Accept(H)=Q implies k(H)>=1.
```

For a long incoming helix (`h>=3`):

- with no `Q` child, every endpoint composition is `F`;
- with one `Q` child, every endpoint composition is `F`;
- with two `Q` children (possible only at `M2/M3` and only when `k=2`),
  `h=3` gives `Q`, while `h=4` and `h>=5` give `F`.

For a short incoming helix (`h=2`), its children have total short count at most
one:

- `E`, `M2`, and `M3` with at most one `Q` child give `F`;
- `L0` and `L1` give `Q`.

Those statements are the exhaustive product of four helix length classes,
five endpoint types, and every `F/Q` child multiset permitted by the counter.
They can also be checked directly from the tables in the theory files.  They
prove preservation of `I`.  The witnesses in `SMALLEST_WITNESSES.md` prove that
no listed state is spurious.

Because there are only 64 possible subsets at each of three counter values,
least-fixed-point iteration terminates.  The independent verifier recomputes
the fixed point from the defining colour algebra rather than accepting this
two-state summary as input.

## 5. Exact root acceptance

Every reachable state, `F` or `Q`, offers both `B` and `W` at `xi`.

- If the root has an unpaired child, `xi=0` is forced and its degree is at most
  two.  Assign `B` at degree one and `BW` at degree two.
- If the root has no unpaired child and degree is one or two, make the same
  choice `xi=0`.
- If the root has no unpaired child and degree is three or four, choose
  `eta=0` (`xi=1`).  At `eta`, an `F` child accepts any colour and a `Q` child
  forces `G`.  Since `Q` consumes at least one short helix, K2 permits at most
  two `Q` children.  For degree three use `BWG` with at most one `Q`, and `BGG`
  or `WGG` with two.  For degree four use `BWGG`.

Thus every reachable total-counter-two root tuple is accepted.  The certificate
exhausts 2, 5, 5, and 5 unordered `(counter,state)` child multisets at root
degrees 1,2,3,4 respectively.

This is a universal closure argument over a complete finite grammar, not an
inference from nucleotide-length-bounded enumeration.

## 6. Negative control and boundary of this proof

With three short helices, three `Q` subtrees may be direct children of an
unpaired-free degree-3 root.  At `xi` they offer only `B,W`, so three ports
exceed the two non-grey slots.  At `eta` they force `GGG`, exceeding grey
capacity two.  The concrete target

```text
((.))((.))((.))
```

realizes this rejected state.  This validates that the automaton can return
NO.  It does not prove that three short helices are a universal sharp boundary;
it is one exact out-of-class obstruction to strong 2-separation.

## 7. Logical verdicts supported here

- **A (one specified deterministic extension):** outside this adaptive exact
  automaton; the frozen policy and its Type-I failure are analyzed separately.
- **B (existence of a proper strong 2-separated colouring):** universally YES
  for K2 by the exact closure above.
- **C (ordinary separated colouring):** follows YES from B for K2.
- **D (unique designability):** follows once the already machine-checked
  colouring-to-design reduction is invoked; that reduction is not reproved in
  this local automaton file.

## 8. Reproduction

From the repository root, with standard-library Python 3.11 or newer:

```bash
python3 automaton/verify_certificate.py
python3 automaton/mutation_tests.py
```

The first command reconstructs all valid helix words through the two parity
seeds, checks the exact relations and append-two lemma, recomputes the least
fixed point, exhausts all K2 root-state multisets, verifies both CSVs, and
rejects the three-`Q` control.  The second executes the four load-bearing
mutations and prints deterministic JSON.  Neither command uses a third-party
package, a random seed, or network access.
